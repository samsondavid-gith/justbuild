Ideation  ·  1 of 6

Find an idea that holds up.

Session 1 · 90 min  |  Model: Gemini (AI Studio, free) - fast, for many ideas

You want a lot of ideas fast, then one that survives three lenses. Use a quick model here, not a heavy one.

Prompt A - get 15 raw ideas
I am a college student in [city]. I want to start a tiny business I could build in two days using AI, aimed at people around me [e.g. my hostel, my campus, my neighbourhood]. Give me 15 specific ideas. For each, in one line: WHO it is for (a real person, not "everyone"), the annoying MOMENT it fixes, and how they solve it TODAY. No generic ideas like "a food app" - be specific and local.
Prompt B - pressure-test one idea (run for your top 2-3)
Here is my idea: [paste your idea]. Pressure-test it honestly in three lenses: - HUMANICS: name one real person and the exact moment they feel this pain. What do they do today instead? - MECHANICS: can an AI-built app actually finish the core of this by tomorrow evening? What is the ONE screen that matters? - VALUENOMICS: would anyone pay even a small amount? What is the very first rupee? Be blunt. If it fails a lens, say so, and tell me the smallest change that would fix it.

How to use it

  1. Run Prompt A, read all 15, pick your 3 favourites (quantity first, no judging yet).
  2. Run Prompt B on each of your 3. Weak ideas die here, cheaply, on paper.
  3. Commit to the ONE idea that survived all three lenses. Write it on the board as: "For [person], who [pain] at [moment], I build [thing] so they [outcome]."
  4. Clock test: can AI finish the core by tomorrow evening? If not, subtract until it can.
Marry the problem, date the idea. If the idea fails a lens, keep the person and the pain, change the solution.
